# System Verification Design

This document describes the system-level verification strategy for CanvasNetVegaLite.

## Verification Approach

`CanvasNetVegaLite` is verified at three tiers. The system tier, described here, exercises the library purely
through its public API from the perspective of a consumer: it opens original-work Vega-Lite specifications from
in-memory text, streams, and the fixture files with `VegaLiteDocument.Parse` and `VegaLiteDocument.Open`, supplies
`VegaLiteOpenOptions`, `VegaLiteRenderOptions`, `VegaLiteFontOptions`, and `VegaLiteLimits`, measures documents with
`VegaLiteDocument.Measure`, renders them with `VegaLiteDocument.Render`, and asserts on observable outputs: the
`VegaLiteDocument` description properties (`SourceName`, `SchemaUrl`, `SchemaMajorVersion`, `Title`, `MarkTypes`,
`DataReferences`, `ParameterNames`), the `VegaLiteLocation` carried by a `VegaLiteSpecException` or a
`VegaLiteRenderException`, the events delivered to the warning and unsupported-feature handlers (their stable
`vl-*` identifiers, JSON pointers, and one-based line and column), the `VegaLiteSize` returned by `Measure`, the
pixels of the rendered `Surface`, and the exception types thrown for invalid arguments and hostile input. No
mocking or stubbing is used. The subsystem tier (see the _SpecModel_, _Data_, _Expressions_, _Transforms_, _Scales_,
_Guides_, _Layout_, _Marks_, and _Scenegraph_ Subsystem Verification Designs under `canvas-net-vega-lite/`) verifies the
units of each internal subsystem working together, and the unit tier verifies each unit in isolation (see the
_VegaLiteDocument Unit Verification Design_ and the unit verification designs under `canvas-net-vega-lite/spec-model/`,
`canvas-net-vega-lite/data/`, `canvas-net-vega-lite/expressions/`, `canvas-net-vega-lite/transforms/`,
`canvas-net-vega-lite/scales/`, `canvas-net-vega-lite/guides/`, `canvas-net-vega-lite/layout/`,
`canvas-net-vega-lite/marks/`, and `canvas-net-vega-lite/scenegraph/`), including the internal types made visible to the
test assembly through `InternalsVisibleTo`.

The open and parse scenarios assert on what a document reports about its source: that the three entry points
(string, stream, and path) agree, including the rendered pixels; that schema versions 5 and 6 are both read with
the Vega-Lite 6 defaults while other versions produce a stable warning and malformed schema values are
specification errors; that malformed JSON fails at an exact file, line, and column; and that hostile input
(oversize source, excessive nesting, a stream that never ends) fails closed with a typed exception that callers can
catch. The error contract is part of what is verified: `VegaLiteSpecException` carries a `VegaLiteLocation`,
oversize source throws a plain `InvalidDataException` because no source location is meaningful for it, and invalid
arguments throw `ArgumentException` types that name the offending parameter.

The rendering scenarios assert on what measuring and rendering a document produces, with analytic expectations
only: nothing is compared with a golden image and nothing is generated by another implementation. The empty view
has the documented 310 by 310 size (the Vega-Lite 6 default view of 300 by 300 plus 5 pixels of padding per side),
a white background, and a one-pixel `#ddd` border at exact pixel columns; a title grows the canvas by the title
height plus its offset; the bar fixture's canvas size, bar colors at the bar centers, grid lines, domain line,
tick marks, and title ink are asserted at pixel positions derived from the layout formulas; the scatter, rule, and
tick fixtures are asserted by sample pixels whose blended colors are computed from the closed form of source-over
compositing at the default mark opacity of 0.7; `Measure` equals the size of the surface that `Render` returns, with
and without a scale; `Render(width, height)` fits the chart uniformly and centers it on the background; a
container-sized chart is laid out again for the container width; and a caller configuration replaces the
specification's own configuration.

The safety scenarios assert on what the system does with hostile or unsupported input: constructs that cannot be
drawn yet (marks, channels, the transforms and expression constructs of later phases, compositions, remote data) are
reported once each, in document order, on the calling thread, with their stable identifier and location, while the
supported part of the chart is still drawn; strict mode stops at the first unsupported feature after delivering it;
remote data is reported and never fetched or resolved; every resource limit fails closed with a located exception;
cancellation is honored before any event is delivered; and repeated and concurrent renders of one immutable document are
byte-identical. The font scenario proves that, by default, only the bundled Liberation fonts are used, whatever
fonts the machine has installed.

The expression, transform, and parameter scenarios assert on what the system computes from the `transform` array and
the `params` of the drawn view, and how it fails: the row transforms `calculate`, `filter`, `fold`, `flatten`,
`sample`, `pivot`, and `lookup` decide which dots are drawn and where, on 100 by 100 pixel charts whose positions are
derived by hand from a scale domain of 0 to 10; the static parameters, their defaults, their dependencies, and the
caller's overrides reach the transforms and the row-bound mark values; the expressions of the mark properties and the
color and size values are evaluated against the transformed row of each mark; the constructs that the engine does not
run (unknown functions, view state, bitwise operators, selections, and the transforms of later phases) are reported
at the text of the expression or the transform, never executed, and in strict mode stop the render at the first one;
an expression with a syntax error, one over the length or nesting limit, an unknown parameter, and an invalid
parameter definition fail when the document is opened with the location of the offending text and the offset inside
it; hostile evaluation (a step budget that is too small, a catastrophic regular expression, a fold over the row limit,
a lookup that leaves the sandbox) fails closed with a located exception or finishes safely; and a chart through every
kind of transform measures the size it renders and renders the same bytes on every call and from many threads.

The aggregation scenarios assert on what the system computes from the aggregating transforms and from the `aggregate`,
`bin`, `stack`, and `sort` properties of an encoding, with expectations derived by hand from the scale formulas: the
sums per category of the `aggregate-bars` fixture become three bars in first-seen order with a sorted band scale, a
domain of 0 to 7, and Vega-Lite's default titles (`Sum of units`); the twelve weights of the `histogram` fixture are
binned with ten bins asked for into the bins 0 to 35 by 5 and counted (3, 2, 3, 1, 1, 1, 1), and each bar fills its bin
less a pixel of spacing and the half pixel of the axis translate (x = 1, 41, and so on, 39 pixels wide); the
`stacked-bars` fixture stacks its layers from the bottom in descending order of the color on `y` (north: washers 0 to
5, nuts 5 to 7, bolts 7 to 10; south: nuts 0 to 1, bolts 1 to 5), so the y axis reaches the total 10 and the layers are
drawn between their stack bounds, with a pixel inside a lower layer in its color; the `binned-ranges` fixture draws data
that is binned already as bars over their ranges; and the `window-cumulative` fixture draws the running total of a
window transform (4, 7, 12, 14). The scenarios also assert that the groups count in the row budget (the five rows and
three groups of the aggregate bars need `MaxDataRows` 8), that the marks of stacked and binned charts are counted
exactly by the scene budget, that measuring equals rendering and renders are byte-identical for every fixture, and that
an aggregate, bin, or stack is drawn without a report while a part that is still unsupported next to them (a binned time
unit) is reported once and stops a strict render.

The time scenarios assert on what the system draws from temporal fields, time units, and the `TimeZone` render option,
with expectations derived by hand from the calendar of 2024 (a leap year that starts on a Monday), the d3 time interval
table, Vega's multi-format, and the layout of the chart, and never from the output of a real Vega-Lite: three points on
January 1, 6, and 11 sit at 0, 150, and 300 pixels of 300 on a time axis whose ticks are every second day and whose
labels are `2024`, `Wed 03`, `Fri 05`, `Jan 07`, `Tue 09`, and `Thu 11`; the bars of three months span the 91 days of
January 1 to April 1 (the bar of January starts at 1 and is 31 x 300 / 91 - 1 = 101.198 wide); a zone 5:30 hours ahead of
UTC moves a reading at 20:00Z to the next day, which shifts its point by exactly one day of the scale; in a zone that
follows the United States rule the day of March 10, 2024 is 23 hours long, so the middle of three days is 240 pixels in
of 470 instead of 235; a UTC time unit ignores the zone; a parse directive reads day-first dates that the default parse
rejects; text dates are parsed like `Date.parse`; a filter by time unit and date-time object keeps four of eight rows; a
temporal color has a gradient legend of dates; an ordinal time unit is a band scale; a binned time unit and a band
position other than one half on a bar are reported and stop a strict render; the tick and string limits and the
cancellation fail closed; and the renders are byte-identical on repeated and concurrent calls, under other cultures, and
whatever the host's time zone is. The units under it are verified in the _TimeCalendar_, _TimeFormat_, _TimeLabels_, and
_TimeUnitTransform_ Unit Verification Designs.

The line, area, trail, text, and arc scenarios assert on what the system draws from the path and polar marks, with
expectations derived by hand from the scale formulas and the path rules, and never from the output of a real Vega-Lite
or d3-shape: a quantitative axis that starts at zero maps the three points (0, 0), (1, 10), and (2, 5) of a 300 by 200
chart to the pixels (0, 200), (150, 0), and (300, 100), so the line is the three commands `M0,200 L150,0 L300,100` in the
first color of the categorical scheme with a stroke of width 2, and the area of the same data is that outline forward
and the baseline y = 200 backward (`M0,200 L150,0 L300,100 L300,200 L150,200 L0,200 Z`); two stacked series of ten share
the domain 0 to 20 so the first lies on top (pixels 0 to 100) and the second below (100 to 200); a trail of the sizes 4,
8, and 12 has the half widths 2, 4, and 6 and so covers 4, 8, and 12 rows of pixels; a null in the middle of five points
breaks the line into two subpaths (`M0,200 L75,0 M225,100 L300,40`) and the filter mode connects its neighbors; an impute
adds the missing key of a series (the third vertex (150, 200) of the second line) and the implicit impute of a stacked
area gives a layer a zero-thick vertex; the slices of the weights 1, 1, and 2 of a 200 by 200 pie are the angles 0 to a
quarter turn, a quarter to a half, and a half to a whole turn around (100, 100) with the outer radius 100, whose pixel
counts are the areas of the wedges (a quarter of pi times 100 squared, and the same less the hole of a donut with the
inner radius 50), and their labels at the radius 60 sit at the middles of the slices, (142.4264, 57.5736), (142.4264,
142.4264), and (40, 100). The scenarios also assert that all fifteen interpolations draw finite coordinates (the area of
a bundle curve is reported and drawn linear), that the point and line overlays are drawn in the order area, line, point,
that the `order` channel decides the path and the overlap of marks, that the constructs that are still unsupported (the
geoshape and image marks, the `show` invalid mode, the sizes of an area, and so on) are reported once and in order while
the chart is drawn, that a path, an arc, and a label above the limits fail closed with a located exception before they are
created, that a canceled token stops every one of the marks, and that repeated, concurrent, scaled, and other-culture
renders are identical. The units under them are verified in the _CurveBuilder_ and _ArcGeometry_ Unit Verification Designs
and in the _MarkBuilder_ and _ScenePainter_ Unit Verification Designs.

The data scenarios assert on what the system reads and how it fails: a specification opened from a file reads its
relative data file from its own folder, and the base directory, a data path, and a resolver callback find the same
file for a specification that has no folder, each giving the pixels of the chart whose rows are written inline; a
document with no folder, base directory, data path, or callback fails to render a local file with a located
`VegaLiteRenderException` rather than drawing an empty chart; a missing file, a path that leaves its root, a colon in a
path, and bytes that are not UTF-8 are located failures in lenient and strict mode alike, while an absolute path is
remote data that is reported and never opened; sequences and named datasets (the caller's over the specification's)
draw the pixels of the same rows inline without any report; values that an explicit `parse` cannot read are delivered
as one `vl-data-parse-failed` warning per field after the reports of the specification; every call reads its file again;
and the data limits fail before the scene limit is reached. The sandbox mechanics (links, open failures, bounded
reads) are
proven once at the core `SandboxedFileResolver` unit with an injected link probe and open seam; this system proves that
each refusal reaches the caller as a located error, with real links as additional evidence at the unit tier.

System tests reside in `VegaLiteSystemIntegrationTests.cs` (the single-concern scenarios, one per requirement
topic), `VegaLiteChartIntegrationTests.cs` (the fixture-driven chart scenarios),
`VegaLiteAggregationIntegrationTests.cs` (the aggregation, binning, stacking, and window scenarios), and
`VegaLiteTimeIntegrationTests.cs` (the time scenarios), `VegaLiteLineAreaIntegrationTests.cs` (the line, area, trail,
curve, overlay, invalid data, impute, draw order, and limit scenarios), and `VegaLiteArcTextIntegrationTests.cs` (the arc,
text, unsupported, and determinism scenarios of the path marks) within the
`DemaConsulting.CanvasNet.VegaLite.Tests` project; the shared helpers (a pixel snapshot, uniform-color and
region checks, ink bounds, and a data-reference projection) are in `VegaLiteTestHelpers.cs`, and the data helpers
(temporary directories and virtual file sets) in `Data/DataTestSupport.cs`. The fixtures in
`Fixtures/` are original works, authored for this repository without reference to any Vega-Lite example, gallery
chart, documentation snippet, or `vega-datasets` data set; their provenance and the rules for adding fixtures are
documented in `Fixtures/README.md` beside them.

## Test Environment

- **Framework**: xUnit v3 running under the .NET SDK
- **Execution**: `dotnet test` invoked by `build.ps1` and the CI pipeline, on every target framework
  (`net8.0`, `net9.0`, `net10.0`) and on the Windows, Linux, and macOS CI runners
- **Dependencies**: No external services, databases, or network access required. The twenty-seven fixture files in
  `test/DemaConsulting.CanvasNet.VegaLite.Tests/Fixtures/` (`bar-inline.vl.json`, `point-inline.vl.json`,
  `rule-tick-inline.vl.json`, `titles-config.vl.json`, `unsupported.vl.json`, the data-file chart `data-files.vl.json`
  with its `data-files.csv`, the nested JSON rows `data-values.json`, the aggregation charts
  `aggregate-bars.vl.json`, `histogram.vl.json`, `stacked-bars.vl.json`, `signed-stacks.vl.json`,
  `binned-ranges.vl.json`, and `window-cumulative.vl.json`, the legend, scale, and encoding charts
  `legend-symbols.vl.json`, `legend-merged.vl.json`, `legend-gradient.vl.json`, `log-scales.vl.json`,
  `binned-channels.vl.json`, and `conditional-marks.vl.json`, and the time charts `time-points.vl.json`,
  `time-bars.vl.json`, `time-zone.vl.json`, `time-filter.vl.json`, `time-legend.vl.json`, and `time-parse.vl.json`
  with its `time-parse.csv`, plus a provenance `README.md`) are
  copied to the test output directory and located relative to the test assembly. The time fixtures are original works
  with made-up dates: `time-points` (three readings on January 1, 6, and 11, 2024), `time-bars` (six visits in the
  months January to March 2024), `time-zone` (three readings, one at 2024-03-05T20:00Z), `time-filter` (eight readings
  across 2023 and 2024), `time-legend` (three samples whose color is the date), and `time-parse` (three day-first dates
  `05/03/2024`, `15/03/2024`, and `25/03/2024` in the CSV file). The line, area, trail, text, and arc scenarios use
  inline specifications only (charts of 300 by 200 and of 200 by 200 pixels with made-up numbers) and add no fixture
  file; `unsupported.vl.json` names a `geoshape` mark, a mark that is reported permanently
- **Fonts**: Rendering uses the Liberation fonts bundled with the core `DemaConsulting.CanvasNet` package; no
  installed font is required, and the font scenario proves that installed fonts are not consulted by default
- **Time zone and culture**: Output does not depend on the machine's time zone or culture. The default time zone
  of the render options is UTC, the time scenarios build their zones from `TimeZoneInfo.CreateCustomTimeZone` (a fixed
  +05:30 zone, a zone with the United States daylight saving rule, and a zone with a zero offset) and never read the
  host's zone, and number and date formatting use fixed English names, separators, and the Unicode minus sign (the
  culture scenarios render under `tr-TR` and `de-DE`)
- **Threads**: The determinism scenario renders one document from sixteen concurrent tasks and compares each
  result with a single-threaded baseline, and the transforms scenario does the same from twelve tasks; the
  handler-thread scenario records the managed thread on which each handler ran and compares it with the caller's
- **Isolation**: Each test method builds its own sources, options, and streams; no shared mutable state between
  tests. The path-based scenarios read the checked-in fixture files; the tests that need a file of their own (the
  path entry point, the font directory, and the lookup sandbox scenarios) create it in a uniquely named per-test
  temporary folder and delete the folder when they finish

## External Interface Simulation

The external interfaces of the system are the file system (the `VegaLiteDocument.Open(string)` overload, the data
files that `Measure` and `Render` read from the base directory and the data paths, and the font directories), streams
(the `VegaLiteDocument.Open(Stream)` overload and the stream a file callback returns),
the network (never used: scheme-bearing data URLs are reported and not fetched), the installed fonts (not consulted
by default), and the caller's handler delegates. Nothing is simulated: file input uses the real fixture files copied
to the output directory and real files in per-test temporary directories, the file callback is a real delegate over an
in-memory file set that records every logical path it is asked for, stream input uses real `MemoryStream` instances
over fixture or generated bytes, and the
one hostile stream (an endless stream of blanks that counts the bytes handed to the reader) is a real
`Stream` subclass local to the tests. The handlers are real delegates that record what they receive. The
never-fetched property of remote data is verified by asserting that scheme-bearing URLs are reported, that the
document still renders, and that a file resolver callback that records its calls is never asked for anything. The
core `Surface`, `Rgba32`, `TrueTypeFont`, and the core rasterization units are used for real in every render
scenario (the pixels are read back from the returned surface); no mocking framework is used.

## System-Level Test Scenarios

### Open: String, Stream, And Path Entry Points Agree

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Open_ParseStringOpenStreamAndOpenPathAgree`

Opens the same specification text as a string with `VegaLiteDocument.Parse`, as a UTF-8 stream with a byte-order
mark with `VegaLiteDocument.Open(Stream)`, and as a file with `VegaLiteDocument.Open(string)`. Asserts that every
public description (schema URL and major version, title, mark types, data references with kind, URL, row count,
pointer, line, and column, and parameter names) is the same; that the path names the source and its folder is the
data base directory while the other two documents are unnamed; that the stream is left open and fully consumed;
that explicit open options win over the defaults; and that the three documents render the same pixels.

### Schema: Versions 5 And 6 Use Version 6 Defaults

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Schema_V5AndV6UseV6Defaults`

Renders the same specification declared with a version 5 and a version 6 `$schema` and asserts that both are
accepted silently and render identically with the Vega-Lite 6 defaults (a natural size of 310 by 310). Asserts that
another major version (4) renders the same pixels but is reported once with the stable `vl-schema-version` warning
at `/$schema`, that a document without a schema renders the same, and that a non-string schema, an empty schema, and
a plain Vega schema are specification errors whose location points at `/$schema`.

### Options: Validated And Immutable

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Options_AreValidatedAndImmutable`

Asserts that no public property of `VegaLiteOpenOptions`, `VegaLiteRenderOptions`, `VegaLiteFontOptions`, or
`VegaLiteLimits` can be set after initialization, that collections are read-only views over private copies, that
invalid values fail at initialization naming the property rather than later during rendering, and that a parameter
override the document does not declare is rejected when it is used.

### Spec Errors: Malformed JSON Reports Line And Column

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Spec_MalformedJsonReportsLineAndColumn`

Parses a specification with a missing value on its third line and asserts a `VegaLiteSpecException` whose location
carries the source name, line 3, and column 11, a message that begins with the text `broken.vl.json:3:11:`, and that
the exception is assignable to `FormatException` (it is a `FormatException`, not an `InvalidDataException`).

### Render: Empty View Has Documented Size And Colors

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_EmptyTitleViewHasDocumentedSizeAndColors`

Renders a view without axes and asserts a 310 by 310 surface (the 300 by 300 default data rectangle of Vega-Lite 6
plus 5 pixels of padding per side) on an opaque white background, that `Measure` reports what `Render` produces,
that the one-pixel `#ddd` border of the plot area covers exactly the pixel column and row at its edge (column and
row 5, and 305), that a caller background replaces white everywhere outside the border, and that a title grows the
canvas above the plot area by the title height plus its offset without stopping rendering.

### Render: Scale And Explicit Size Fit

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_ScaleAndExplicitSizeFit`

Asserts that `VegaLiteRenderOptions.Scale` multiplies the natural size (rounded to whole pixels) and that `Measure`
agrees; that `Render(width, height)` honors an explicit size exactly, fits the chart uniformly, centers it in the
surface, and fills the margins with the background, with the scale having no influence on an explicit size; and
that invalid sizes are rejected before any other work, with no event delivered and cancellation not consulted.

### Measure: Equals The Natural Rendered Size

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Measure_MatchesNaturalRenderedSize`

Measures and renders every chart fixture with and without a scale and asserts that the measured size equals the
size of the rendered surface, that the scale multiplies it and rounds to whole pixels, and that the fixtures have
different natural sizes, so the size does depend on the chart (data, axes, and title). This is the system proof that
measuring and rendering share one layout.

### Render With Width And Height: Uniform Fit And Centering

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_WidthHeightUniformlyFitsAndCenters`

Renders the 289 by 264 bar fixture into a 600 by 300 and a 300 by 600 surface and asserts that the sizes are exact,
that the margins are the background, that the height limits the scale to 300 / 264 in the first case (a chart 328.4
pixels wide, centered with about 135.8 pixels of margin on each side) and the width limits it to 300 / 289 in the
second, that the ink is centered in both directions within the few pixels of padding asymmetry of the chart, and
that the East bar center is the mark color at the scaled and offset position.

### Render: Container Width Fills The Container

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_ContainerWidthFillsTheContainer`

Renders a chart whose width is `"container"` in a 400 pixel container and asserts that the canvas width is exactly
the container width, that the data rectangle is what the padding and the left guides leave (400 - 10 - 38.68 =
351.32 pixels, which makes the canvas 400.32 pixels, rounded to 400), that `Measure` agrees, and that the four
bands share the data rectangle (the East bar spans 351.32 / 4 * 0.9 = 79 pixels of the 87.8 pixel step).

### Charts: Bar Chart Has Expected Sample Pixels

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_InlineBarHasExpectedSamplePixels`

Renders `bar-inline.vl.json` (four categories in a 240 by 200 data rectangle, a custom y title, a chart title) and
asserts, at positions derived by hand from the band and linear scale formulas, the canvas size (origin plus 240 by
200, plus the 32 pixels the x axis needs below and the 5 pixels of padding), the bar colors at the bar centers
(bands of 54 pixels starting at 3, 63, 123, and 183, rising to y = 114, 144, 90, and 18), the transparent view
above a bar and in the gaps, the `#ddd` grid lines at 160, 120, 80, 40, and 0, the `#888` domain line along y = 200
and tick below the East band center, and the ink of the title and the axis texts in their bands, with the padding
untouched.

### Charts: Points, Rules, And Ticks Have Expected Sample Pixels

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_PointRuleTickHaveExpectedSamplePixels`

Renders `point-inline.vl.json` and `rule-tick-inline.vl.json` (and variants that swap the mark for `rule` and
thicken the ticks). Asserts the scatter points at x = 300 (h - 6) / 14 and y = 200 - 2 yield in their blended
colors (blue `(76, 120, 168)` over white at 0.7 opacity is `(129.7, 160.5, 194.1)`; orange `(245, 133, 24)` is
`(248, 169.6, 93.3)`), that the row without a y value is not drawn, that rule stems are drawn from x(v) back to x(0)
at the band centers, 2 pixels thick, as opaque black (rules are not made translucent), and that a tick covers its
band of 30 pixels (inner padding 0.25 over three bands of 40) in blue at 0.7 opacity while the pixel just outside is
white.

### Configuration: Caller Configuration Overrides The Specification

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Render_CallerConfigOverridesSpecificationConfig`

Renders `titles-config.vl.json`, whose own configuration sets the tick color, with a caller configuration that
turns ticks off and asserts that the guides need less room (the canvas is smaller), proving that the precedence is
Vega-Lite defaults, then the specification's `config`, then `VegaLiteRenderOptions.Config`.

### Unsupported: Constructs Are Reported With Pointer And Location

**Tests**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Unsupported_MarkIsReportedWithPointer`,
`CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Unsupported_MarkAndTransformCarryPointerLineColumn`

Renders a document with a schema version 4 declaration (a warning first) and a layer of two views, the second with
an unsupported mark, and asserts the exact ordered list of reports (`vl-schema-version`, `vl-composition-layer`,
`vl-mark-geoshape`), that each is delivered once, in document order, to the right handler, on the calling thread
(the recorded managed thread identifiers all equal the caller's), and that a supported `bar` mark in the same
document is silent. Renders `unsupported.vl.json` (a `regression` transform, a `geoshape` mark, and a `bandPosition`) and
asserts exactly one report per construct, in document order, with its stable identifier, JSON pointer, and one-based
line and column of the value (`vl-transform-regression` at `/transform/0`, line 12, column 5; `vl-mark-geoshape` at `/mark`,
line 14, column 11; and `vl-encoding-bandposition` at `/encoding/y/bandPosition`, line 17, column 71), each in the file
`unsupported.vl.json`, and that the geoshape mark is not drawn, so no data, scale, or axis work is done for it and the view
is empty with the default size.

### Strict Mode: Throws At The First Unsupported Feature

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Strict_ThrowsAtFirstUnsupportedFeature`

Renders a single view with an unsupported mark (`geoshape`, `vl-mark-geoshape` at `/mark`) first and an unsupported
channel second with
`ThrowOnUnsupportedFeature` and asserts a `VegaLiteUnsupportedFeatureException` for the first feature only, with
its location, that the handler saw exactly that report, that a handler that fails while the failure is being
reported does not hide it, that after a successful render a failing handler propagates, and that on
`unsupported.vl.json` strict mode stops at the first construct in the text (the `regression` transform,
`vl-transform-regression` at `/transform/0` on line 12).

### Data: Remote URLs Are Reported And Never Fetched

**Test**: `CanvasNetVegaLite_SystemIntegration_Data_RemoteUrlIsReportedNotFetched`

Parses a layered specification whose views have an `https` URL, a `file` URL, and empty inline data, with a
`FileResolver` callback that records every name it is asked for. Asserts that each scheme-bearing URL is reported as
`vl-data-remote-url` at the pointer of its `data` object (`/layer/0/data` and `/layer/1/data`), that the composition
itself is also reported (`vl-composition-layer`), that the empty inline data warns with `vl-data-empty` at
`/layer/2/data`, that rendering still succeeds in lenient mode with empty datasets, that strict mode throws for the
first report (and for a single view with remote data, `vl-data-remote-url` at `/data`), and that the callback was never
asked for anything. The classification of every other scheme-bearing form (UNC, drive-letter, and
protocol-relative paths) is verified at the unit tier in the _FeatureCatalog Unit Verification Design_.

## Acceptance Criteria

A system-level test run passes when every scenario above passes without error or unexpected exception, on every
target framework. Every requirement in `docs/reqstream/canvas-net-vega-lite.yaml` and
`docs/reqstream/canvas-net-vega-lite/platform-requirements.yaml` links to at least one passing test listed above.
Any unexpected exception, wrong exception type (in particular a `VegaLiteSpecException` where an
`InvalidDataException` is documented, or the reverse), wrong location, wrong reported identifier, a measured size
that differs from the rendered size, a pixel outside its analytic tolerance, output that differs between
repeated or concurrent renders, an empty chart for a local data file that cannot be used, a request that reaches the
file callback for remote data, a dot that a transform or a parameter should have placed (or removed) at another pixel,
an expression construct that is executed although it is reported as unsupported, a hostile expression or transform that
does not fail closed with the location of its text, a path, wedge, or label that is not where its derivation puts it, a
curve with a coordinate that is not finite, a path, arc, or label that is built beyond a limit, or any read outside the
sandbox constitutes a failure.
